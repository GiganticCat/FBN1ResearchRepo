# Project Proposal: Structural and Biophysical Characterization of Pathogenic FBN1 Missense Variants

Mutations in *FBN1*, the gene encoding the extracellular glycoprotein fibrillin-1, are the principal cause of Marfan syndrome, an autosomal dominant connective tissue disorder whose most severe manifestation is aortic dissection. The majority of pathogenic *FBN1* variants are missense substitutions concentrated in the 43 calcium-binding EGF-like (cbEGF) domains, where they are thought to act through two distinct structural mechanisms: disruption of the six conserved cysteines that form each domain's three disulfide bonds, and disruption of the acidic/amide residue consensus that coordinates a stabilizing Ca²⁺ ion. This project asks whether pathogenic variants are enriched at these two classes of structurally critical residue relative to benign variants and to the background of all possible missense substitutions, and, more importantly, quantifies how much each mechanism actually perturbs calcium coordination geometry, disulfide integrity, and folding stability using structural and biophysical measurement rather than sequence-based inference alone. The approach integrates variant classifications from ClinVar, population frequencies from gnomAD v4, orthogonal pathogenicity scores from AlphaMissense, and domain annotations from UniProt (canonical isoform P35555, MANE transcript NM_000138.5) into a single validated variant master table, with every genomic position mapped to structural coordinates via SIFTS. Calcium sites were sourced from experimental cbEGF structures where available and from AlphaFold3 cofolding elsewhere, calibrated against the experimental structures and independently validated with CheckMyMetal geometry analysis before being trusted for uncrystallized domains. FoldX was used to predict the folding free-energy cost (ΔΔG) of each variant with structural coverage, alongside solvent accessibility and disulfide-partner distance, giving a physics-based readout that is independent of any clinical classification scheme.

To date, Phases 0 through 5 of the planned seven-phase pipeline are complete, with every intermediate validation gate passing, including self-tests confirming each gate actually fails on corrupted or mislabeled input. The variant collection and normalization stages yielded 4,451 wild-type-verified missense variants with zero identity mismatches against the reference sequence, and labeled pathogenic, benign, and VUS sets built from thresholds fixed in advance of analysis. The calcium-coordination consensus and cysteine/disulfide annotations were derived empirically from measured Ca²⁺ ligand positions in deposited structures rather than assumed from the literature, and independently reproduce the ClinGen FBN1 VCEP's published motif and cysteine count. The central finding to emerge from the completed structural and statistical analysis is that the two pathogenic mechanisms are biophysically dissociable: cysteine-removing variants substantially destabilize the fold (median ΔΔG 4.19 kcal/mol), while calcium-ligand-removing variants leave folding stability essentially unchanged (median ΔΔG 0.76 kcal/mol) despite being scored as highly deleterious by AlphaMissense, indicating that calcium-site variants are pathogenic functionally rather than thermodynamically and would be systematically missed by stability-based predictors alone. Enrichment of pathogenic variants at these critical residues was also confirmed but is reported as supporting rather than headline evidence, since ClinVar's own classifications were partly derived using the same critical-residue rule being tested. The remaining work, Phase 6, consists of generating publication-quality figures (domain-level variant maps, PyMOL renders of representative calcium sites and disulfides, and distribution plots of the ΔΔG and AlphaMissense comparisons) and supplementary tables, none of which requires further external services or mentor hand-offs.
